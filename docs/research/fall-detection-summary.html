Fall detection on Apple, Samsung, Huawei and Garmin wearables

Summary of fall-detection-sdks-and-model-training.md · research compiled 2026-11-02 · primary sources only (vendor docs, papers, official dataset pages). Items the research could not confirm are marked [UNVERIFIED]; reasoning not stated by a source is marked [INFERENCE]. Full citations are in the report.
Bottom line
  • Apple and Samsung let a third-party app receive the watch's own fall event. Huawei and Garmin do not; there you need your own model on raw motion data.
  • Raw accelerometer/gyroscope access exists on all four, with very different background limits.
  • A wrist model is feasible, but no public wrist dataset has real falls, and lab-trained detectors drop sharply on real falls.
  • Synthetic data helps on some tasks but showed no gain in two wrist/watch studies. Use it to pre-train and widen negatives, and validate only on real, per-device data.
  • Plan on collecting a small own dataset on every target watch.

2. Training a wrist fall-detection model

Why the wrist is hard

  • It is the most active limb, so clapping, table slams and sports resemble falls. Apple and Huawei both warn their detectors may misread high-impact activity as a fall.
  • Lab comparisons rank the wrist worst: best wrist sensitivity 97.37% vs 99.96% at the waist (Özdemir 2016).
  • Real falls differ from simulated ones, and lab detectors degrade on them. Thirteen published algorithms scored 57% sensitivity with 3–85 false alarms/day on real falls (Bagalà 2012).
  • Pooled wrist datasets reach recall 90.6% / specificity 96.9%, but cross-dataset F1 peaks near 70% (Fula & Moreno 2024).
  • Record-wise k-fold inflates accuracy by about 10% (up to 16%). Always split by subject.

Recommended model

1 · TriggerAlways-on impact / free-fall / rotation-burst detector at 25–50 Hz.
2 · ClassifierDual-stream gated 1D-CNN on a 4–6 s window, ~20–60k params, int8.
3 · Verifier10–60 s post-impact inactivity / orientation check.
4 · User countdownCancelable countdown (Apple uses 30 s), then contacts.
  • The gated 1D-CNN comes from a May 2026 arXiv preprint (F1 90–93% on five wrist datasets; 12-participant Pixel Watch 3 test). Treat as a strong candidate, not settled.
  • Small Transformers are not recommended: more false positives at deployment.
  • Garmin: stage 1 plus a compiled tree/logistic head only.
  • Window lengths, parameter counts and size figures are [INFERENCE]; tune empirically.

3. Synthetic data generation

Published evidence is real but task-dependent, and weakest for short wrist motions on a watch.

Physics / video / text → virtual IMU

  • Generate motion, attach a virtual wrist IMU, add bias, noise, clipping and quantisation.
  • Tools: IMUSim (GPL-3.0), OpenSim and MuJoCo (Apache-2.0), Isaac Lab (BSD-3).
  • Fall-specific recipe (Tang et al.): multi-camera video → OpenPose → OpenSim IK → simulated IMU → LSTM. Shank ~90%, other placements 60–70%; gyro matching weaker.
  • IMUTube, IMUGPT (video / LLM-text driven) help most with little real data.

Generative models

  • TimeGAN, Diffusion-TS, IMUDiffusion.
  • Low distribution divergence did not mean better classification (Alamgeer 2025).
  • Trained on a few dozen simulated falls, they mostly reproduce the simulation's biases [INFERENCE].

Augmentation

  • Jitter, scaling, rotation, time/magnitude warp (Um et al. 2017).
  • Rotate accel and gyro with the same matrix; keep rotations wrist-plausible (±30° about the forearm).
  • Keep time-warp within ±15%; clip to sensor range.
  • Avoid SMOTE on fall windows; it blends impact timing and posture [INFERENCE].

Sim-to-real gap

  • Domain randomisation (body size, strap slip, surface stiffness, noise).
  • Calibrate with a little real data; pre-train synthetic, fine-tune real.
  • Per-device fine-tune with feedback: F1 88→92% with fewer false positives (Sensors 2025).

License-safe synthetic pipeline

  1. Motion from own mocap/IMU rig, OpenSim/MuJoCo falls with randomised initial conditions, or own staged video → pose → OpenSim IK.
  2. Virtual wrist IMU with mount offset, strap slip, bias/scale/misalignment, noise, clipping, quantisation, then anti-aliased decimation to 25/50 Hz.
  3. Hard negatives: table slams, clapping, door closing, sitting hard, stumble-recover, sports swings, stairs, arm flailing.
  4. Start 1:1 real:synthetic per class and sweep the fraction.
  5. Pre-train on synthetic, fine-tune on real; run a synthetic-only ablation; subject-wise CV plus cross-dataset tests.
  6. Record data lineage and licences for every asset.

4. Cross-device portability and deployment

Vendor conventions differ: Apple reports g; Android and HarmonyOS report m/s² including gravity; Garmin reports milli-g integers. Axis frames and rates differ too (Samsung Sensor SDK 25 Hz, Garmin up to 100 Hz where supported, Apple 50/100/800 Hz, Huawei ≤200 Hz).

  • Canonical format (a convention to adopt, not a vendor standard): float32, accel in g with gravity, gyro in rad/s, right-handed wrist frame (flat face-up watch reads +1 g on z), 50 Hz and 25 Hz grids from timestamps, left-wrist canonical with mirroring for right wrist, per-record metadata.
  • Per-vendor adapter with a flat-watch calibration check (face up, face down, each edge).
  • Train a 25 Hz-native variant rather than naively downsampling 238 Hz data without anti-aliasing.

5. Recommended plan

  1. Fix scope and claims: a wellness fall-alert assistant, not a medical device, pending legal advice. Set a false-alarm budget (e.g. ≤1/user/day) and a minimum scripted-fall sensitivity.
  2. Per-platform feasibility spike: request the Apple entitlement and apply for Samsung partner status early; log raw IMU on each watch; test Huawei screen-off behaviour; call getMaxSampleRate() on Garmin targets.
  3. Build the canonical data layer: adapters, unit/axis/rate normalisation, calibration tests.
  4. Pool real wrist datasets (check licences), resample with anti-aliasing, split by subject plus leave-one-dataset-out.
  5. Train baselines: threshold trigger, RF/XGBoost on features, gated 1D-CNN. Report sensitivity, specificity, PR and false alarms per simulated day.
  6. Add the license-safe synthetic data; keep it only if real-validation improves.
  7. Collect your own per-device dataset: scripted mat falls with a spotter (≥15 subjects, older adults if ethics allow), hard negatives, multi-day free-living wear. On Apple/Samsung, log native fall events beside your model's output as weak labels [INFERENCE].
  8. Per-device fine-tune and quantise, export, verify numerical parity.
  9. On-device validation: latency, battery, trigger rate, ≥2 weeks in shadow mode.
  10. Alert flow: cancel countdown, contact messaging via the phone companion, consent screens, offline fallback.
  11. Field pilot with an "I fell / false alarm" button; retrain on confirmed false positives; regulatory review before wider release.
  12. Maintain: OS updates change sensor and background behaviour; re-run calibration per release.

6. Safety and regulatory

  • Every vendor disclaims: Apple "cannot detect all falls"; Huawei is "not dedicated emergency assistance"; Samsung SDK data is "for fitness and wellness information only"; Garmin makes the developer responsible for FDA/MHRA compliance.
  • [INFERENCE] FDA's General Wellness guidance (reissued 2026-01-06) probably does not cover an alert that summons emergency help; EU MDR Rule 11 applicability depends on intended purpose. The sources read do not settle either. Get counsel.
  • Health data is special-category under GDPR. Apple App Review 1.4.1 demands disclosed methodology for accuracy claims.
  • Avoid double escalation with native Apple/Samsung fall features; escalate to emergency contacts first; make false-alarm burden a release criterion.

7. Open gaps

  • Apple: entitlement approval criteria; whether events fire when system Fall Detection is off; watchOS accelerometer range and gyro limits.
  • Samsung: which Galaxy Watch models/firmware emit FALL_DETECTED; which metrics populate; partner approval timeline and fees.
  • Huawei: fall event in non-public partner APIs; real sensor rates; background sensing rules. Public docs only; some pages not retrievable.
  • Garmin: which devices exceed 25 Hz; whether the sensor listener runs in a background service; gyro units and axes.
  • Data: licences for SisFall, UP-Fall, FallAllD, WEDA-FALL, SmartFall, KFall unconfirmed; no public real-fall wrist dataset; no elderly wrist fall data; no wrist-specific sim-to-real numbers.
  • All vendors: no published latency or energy figures for custom ML on these watches.
