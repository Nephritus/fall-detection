1. How a fall becomes an alert

Rates: stage 1 = final models with Capture-24 negatives at the validation-slice threshold; two-stage = per-fold models of the stage-2 experiment. Mean of 3 seeds. Stage 2 is drawn dashed because with the stillness check it no longer reduces alarms but still drops falls followed by movement.

2. The model

Trained with AdamW and class-weighted binary cross-entropy, early stopping on validation average precision. Each batch is augmented in signal units: random rotation (watch fit), left/right mirror, gain, time stretch, shift and noise, so the model does not learn a dataset's mounting or sensor. Negatives are the lab activities plus 1,500 random 4 s windows from each of 100 Capture-24 participants' everyday life.

6. False alarms, step by step

Without a check, alarms come from ordinary daytime movement (walking, chores, unlabelled time); with it, nearly all of those go and what remains is a hard movement followed by rest (sitting, lying, sleep), which the wrist cannot tell apart from lying on the floor.

Lab recordings stop a few seconds after the fall, so 10–60 s waits cannot be checked at all. Sensors with more noise at rest (UMAFall, WEDA-FALL) rarely pass: the level has to be set on the target watch.

8. Read this before trusting the numbers

  • Simulated falls only. Every sensitivity comes from volunteers falling on mats; real falls are slower, messier and often followed by attempts to get up. Treat detection rates as upper bounds.
  • The stillness check's cost is unmeasured. It removes 98% of free-living alarms after 10 s, but whether people who really fell keep the wrist still for 10–60 s needs new recordings: a minute or more after each fall, with and without getting up, on the target watch.
  • Free-living wear is a research accelerometer (Axivity AX3, Oxford adults, about a day each), not a smartwatch, and not older adults.
  • Seed spread is large: the CNN alone ranges 20–50 alarms/day (± 1 std over 3 seeds). The false-alarm target of a few per week is an assumption.
