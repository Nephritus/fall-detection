Wrist-IMU fall detection baseline: what we built and what we found

Project summary · repository fall-detection · all numbers come from the reports in reports/ (named per section). Falls in every dataset are simulated, so every sensitivity here is optimistic. Nothing is committed or pushed yet.
Where it stands
  • Ranking is good, deployment is not. A small 1D-CNN on the accelerometer separates falls from everyday activity well on unseen datasets (AUROC 0.99, AUPRC 0.94 after augmentation), but real everyday wear sets off far too many alarms.
  • Biggest wins, in order: more source datasets, augmentation (mirror, rotation, time stretch), and real everyday movement as extra negative training data (Capture-24). The lab false-alarm metric fell from 114 to 51 FA/h with augmentation; free-living alarms per day fell by about half with Capture-24 negatives.
  • Measured free-living false alarms (Capture-24, held-out people): 36 per day at the current threshold, 114 per day at the threshold that catches 95% of simulated falls. A usable product needs a few per week [my assumption, not checked against vendor specs].
  • Latest: more negatives per person (3× and 10×) did not help beyond the 1,500-window setting. A second-stage confirmation (context 1 s before to 4 s after the impact) halves alarms again, 35 → 17 per day, for about 5 points of per-recording fall sensitivity (0.89 → 0.85). Still more than an order of magnitude from the target, and its stillness cue may be an artefact of how falls were recorded.
  • Did not help: synthetic mocap data, hard-negative mining on lab data, gyroscope channels, per-device fine-tuning with fewer than ~4 volunteers, more Capture-24 windows per person.

1. Goal and how the work went

Goal: a baseline wrist-IMU fall detector for consumer smartwatches (Apple, Samsung, Huawei, Garmin), trained only on public data, with honest evaluation. It started with a research report on vendor SDK access and model training, then a repository, a grilling session that fixed the design, and a series of experiments.

Everything is converted to one canonical format: 25 Hz, accelerometer in g (clipped at ±4 g), gyroscope in rad/s, one wrist-frame convention (left-wrist; right-wrist data mirrored), mounting aligned per person from walking or standing. Windows are 4 s long with 2 s stride; a fall window must contain the impact moment; other windows of a fall recording are excluded. In total 28,012 windows (4,394 fall, 23,618 ADL). Impact moment: the magnitude-peak fallback was used for 2,038 of 2,566 fall recordings.

3. Model and evaluation protocol

  • Model: 1D-CNN, 3 conv blocks (32/64/128, kernel 5, BN, ReLU, max-pool), global average pool, dropout, one logit. AdamW, class-weighted loss, early stopping on subject-wise validation AUPRC. Accelerometer-only (4 input channels) was kept as the default because adding the gyroscope never clearly helped.
  • Headline protocol: leave-one-dataset-out (LODO): train on three fall datasets, test on the fourth, repeat for each. Also subject-wise 5-fold CV (pooled, optimistic).
  • Metrics: AUROC, AUPRC (average precision), sensitivity and false alarms/h at a threshold chosen for 95% sensitivity on a validation slice, and oracle FA/h at 95% sensitivity (threshold set on the test scores; a ranking diagnostic that does not depend on threshold transfer).

  • The threshold rule's tuned dip level ended up at 1.5 g (above resting gravity), so it degenerates to "impact exceeds about 2–2.5 g": it catches nearly every fall and fires on hopping, jogging and clapping.
  • Hard-negative mining on lab data (retrain with up-weighted false alarms) did nothing. The gyroscope gave no cross-dataset benefit.
  • Adding UMAFall (the 4th dataset) improved the accel-only oracle FA/h on every unseen dataset more than any tuning had.

4.2 Augmentation (reports/baseline_augmented, ablation_acc|imu)

On-the-fly synthetic variants of each training batch: forearm roll ±30° plus tilt, left/right mirror, gain ±10%, time stretch ±10%, shift ±0.2 s, jitter. Validation and test data are never augmented.

Oracle FA/h, accel-only, no augmentation114
Oracle FA/h, accel-only, augmented51
Oracle FA/h, accel + gyro, no augmentation192
Oracle FA/h, accel + gyro, augmented73

Accel-only AUROC rose 0.981 → 0.990 and AUPRC 0.875 → 0.938, better in 11–12 of 12 paired runs. Pooled subject-wise CV barely changed (it was near its ceiling): the gain is in cross-dataset transfer.

Which transform matters (ablation, 5 seeds): mirror is the strongest by far (alone it cuts oracle FA/h from 132 to 72, and dropping it from the full set costs the most), then rotation and time stretch. Gain and jitter are at the noise floor. Run-to-run noise is large (macro std 10–30 FA/h), so only differences above about 15 FA/h are meaningful.

4.4 Per-device fine-tuning (reports/finetune_augmented)

One held-out dataset stands in for a new watch; k volunteers adapt the model (plus calibration volunteers). Accel-only, oracle FA/h at 95% sensitivity, k = 4: zero-shot 49, full fine-tune 28, training from scratch on the same volunteers 170. Sensitivity at the chosen threshold went 0.865 → 0.922 at about the same false alarms. But fine-tuning beat zero-shot in only 45% (k=1), 35% (k=2) and 55% (k=4) of runs on the oracle metric: with fewer than about four volunteers the gain is not reliable. Recalibrating only the threshold costs more false alarms; BatchNorm re-estimation hurt.

No reliable gain. A classifier could tell synthetic windows from real ones (AUROC 0.96 ADL, 0.99 falls; a quick unsaved check), a larger gap than between real datasets. Without augmentation the synthetic data raised AUPRC, but never beat augmented real-only training. Recommendation: keep real data plus augmentation; revisit synthetic data only with a sensor model and more varied motion.

4.6 Making false alarms a real number

The replay metric counts every fired 4 s window of scripted clips, so it neither de-duplicates overlapping windows nor resembles a day of wear.

  1. Event counting (reports/alarms): fired windows merged with a 30 s refractory period. About 40% of the old lodo-pooled replay number was the same alarm counted repeatedly (44.8 → 25.4 events/h). A first daily estimate from assumed activity time budgets gave 26 (val-slice) and 155 (lodo-pooled) alarms/day. This was scenario arithmetic, not measurement.
  2. Measured on Capture-24 (reports/freeliving, 140 people, 5 seeds): 71 alarms/day (val-slice, 95% CI 63–79) and 193 (lodo-pooled, 177–210); every person had at least one alarm per day. Free-living walking fires about 8 events/h against 1–3 in lab clips; sleep is quiet (0.18 events/h). The assumed budget had underestimated the current rule by about 2.7×.
  3. Mounting check: rolling the forearm axis by ±30–60° or assuming the other wrist moved the rate only mildly (70 → 73–81 for val-slice), so the numbers do not hinge on my alignment assumption, though 90–180° rolls raise them to 89–99.

4.10 Second-stage confirmation (reports/stage2/report.md)

Design. Stage 1 is the Capture-24-negatives CNN. Candidates are |a| peaks of at least 2 g. Stage 2 is a gradient-boosted classifier on rotation-invariant context features (motion before and after, stillness, posture change, repeat impacts) from 1 s before to 4 s after the candidate, so an alarm is delayed by 4 s. It trains on lab fall recordings against lab ADL candidates and Capture-24 candidates (people P001–P100). An alarm needs both stages. Leave-one-fall-dataset-out, 3 seeds, scored on the 48 held-out Capture-24 people (1,226 h). The “both” column searches two thresholds, so it is an upper bound on the combination; the practical table is the fair comparison.

  • The stages complement each other. Stage 2 alone is worse than stage 1 on Erciyes (ADL such as sitting down is also followed by stillness) but better on UMAFall and WEDA-FALL.
  • Remaining alarms (events/h, stage 1 + trigger → two-stage): sports 16.5 → 6.8, manual work 4.3 → 2.1, household chores 3.0 → 1.8, walking 3.5 → 1.8, sitting 0.64 → 0.35, sleep 0.10 → 0.06.
  • Threshold transfer returns for stage 2: the validation cut-off loses more falls on held-out Erciyes (0.90 → 0.78) than the 2% it was set for.
  • Main risk. Simulated falls end with the volunteer lying still until the recording stops (median post-impact motion 0.01–0.06 g against 0.3–0.85 g for ADL candidates in most datasets). Real falls followed by moving or getting up could be rejected, so real-fall sensitivity may be lower than shown; this cannot be measured without real falls.

5. What the data does not tell us

  • Simulated falls only. No public wrist dataset has real falls. Published results on real falls are far worse; read every sensitivity as an upper bound. Per-window sensitivity is not per-fall detection.
  • Capture-24 is a research accelerometer (AX3, ±8 g) worn by 151 Oxford adults for about a day each, on the dominant wrist (assumed right). A smartwatch population will differ; a real fall that happened in it would count as a false alarm.
  • Assumptions in the data pipeline: UMAFall's wrist side (taken from the research report), the magnitude-peak impact fallback for Erciyes and UMAFall, per-person mounting alignment (forearm roll weakly constrained), UP-Fall activity codes in the activity mapping (from the dataset's published list, not the files).
  • Run-to-run noise. Training is not bit-reproducible; single folds can differ by up to about 2× between runs with the same seed. Most conclusions rest on 3–8 seeds and paired comparisons.
  • The false-alarm target is my assumption (a few per week). It has not been checked against vendor specifications or regulators.
  • Second stage relies on post-impact stillness, which in the simulated falls may reflect the recording protocol rather than real falls (see 4.10).

6. What is next (recommended order)

  1. Test whether stage 2 survives falls with recovery (no new data). Break stage-2 sensitivity down by fall type: Erciyes has recovery falls (types 905, 906, 914, 916) and WEDA/UP-Fall have different fall types; LAFAN1 “fall and get up” gives mocap examples. If these are rejected, the stillness cue is an artefact.
  2. Measure the stillness ceiling on Capture-24 (cheap): how many candidate impacts are followed by ≥ 4, 10, 30, 60 s of stillness? This bounds how far a longer confirmation window (a “did the wearer stay down?” check) could cut alarms, at the price of a longer delay.
  3. Improve stage 2 where it is cheap to iterate: mine hard negatives for the gradient-boosted model (safer than for the CNN), add posture features (does the arm return to a hanging pose), richer sampling of Capture-24 candidates (it used 600 per person), and a stage-2 threshold rule that transfers.
  4. More people and conditions, not more windows: other free-living wrist cohorts (for example IMU50, 50 people × 5 days, 46.7 GB, not evaluated) and a smartwatch recording, for both stage-1 negatives and stage-2 evaluation.
  5. Collect target-watch data (needed to settle the question): scripted falls with and without recovery plus multi-day wear, then per-device fine-tuning with ≥ 4 volunteers (4.4) and per-user threshold calibration.
  6. Product-level confirmation: a user prompt that cancels on movement, and on Apple and Samsung the OS fall event as a second opinion (the research report found the event exposed for both; not for Huawei or Garmin).
  7. Also pending: deployment feasibility (Core ML or TFLite, latency, vendor sample rates against 25 Hz), a per-fall metric across the board, first commit and push, and moving the loose varsayımlar notes.
